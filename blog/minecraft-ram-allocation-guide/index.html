How much RAM should you give Minecraft? A practical guide

Starting points for Minecraft memory, why more is not always better, and where to set it in the FireClient launcher.

On this page
  1. Quick starting points
  2. Do not give it everything
  3. Set it in FireClient
  4. Signs you need more, or less
  5. Check your result

Giving Minecraft more memory does not always make it faster. Too little causes stutters and crashes. Too much can make garbage collection pauses longer and starves the rest of your PC. This guide gives sensible starting points and shows where the setting lives in FireClient.

Quick starting points

These are rules of thumb, not guarantees. Mods, shaders and view distance change the picture.

  • Vanilla Minecraft 1.21: 2 to 4 GB is usually enough
  • Performance mods on Fabric: 4 GB is a comfortable start
  • Large modpacks or heavy shaders: 6 to 8 GB, raised only if you actually run out

Do not give it everything

Leave at least 2 to 4 GB for Windows and your other apps. If your PC has 8 GB in total, 4 GB for Minecraft is a sensible ceiling. Asking for more than your system can spare pushes memory onto the disk, which is slower than a smaller allocation.

Set it in FireClient

In the launcher settings, choose a memory budget. FireClient passes it to Java as the maximum heap and limits it to a safe range, so an extreme value cannot be set by mistake.

Signs you need more, or less

  • Crashes with an out-of-memory error, or stutters that get worse over time: raise it by 1 GB and test again
  • Short freezes every few seconds on a high setting: try lowering it
  • Slow loading while other apps compete for memory: close them or reduce the amount

Check your result

Change one thing at a time. Keep the FPS and Memory elements on your FireClient HUD while you play for ten minutes, then compare. If you are new to the launcher, start with the install guide.
